Cognitive Psychology Meta-Analysis

Evidence-Based Study Methods Guide

Based on the landmark Dunlosky et al. (2013) meta-analysis in Psychological Science in the Public Interest, evaluating 10 learning techniques.

TIER S

High Utility (Highest Proven Retention)

1. Practice Testing (Active Recall) High Utility

Self-testing or answering flashcards forces the brain to retrieve information from long-term memory, strengthening neural pathways up to 300% more than re-reading.

👉 How to use: Close your notes, write down everything you remember, or answer end-of-chapter problems before looking at the key.

2. Distributed Practice (Spaced Repetition) High Utility

Spreading study sessions over days or weeks instead of massed cramming in one night. It combats the natural Ebbinghaus forgetting curve by triggering consolidation.

👉 How to use: Review new material on Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, and Day 21 in 30-45 minute chunks.

TIER A

Moderate Utility (High Impact for Complex Topics)

3. The Feynman Technique (Self-Explanation) Moderate Utility

Explaining complex concepts out loud in simple, plain language as if teaching a 10-year-old. Immediately highlights where your understanding breaks down.

👉 How to use: Write a 1-paragraph summary without using technical jargon. Re-study the parts you stumble on.

4. Interleaved Practice Moderate Utility

Mixing different problem types or related topics within a single study session rather than doing 50 of the exact same problem in a row.

👉 How to use: In math or physics, shuffle multiple formulas together so you learn *how to choose* the right approach.

TIER D

Low Utility Traps (The "Illusion of Competence")

❌ Highlighting & Underlining

Dunlosky et al. found highlighting often hurts inference skills because students focus on isolated facts instead of concept links.

❌ Passive Re-reading

Re-reading creates visual familiarity, which students mistake for mastery. It produces negligible long-term memory gains.

❌ All-Night Cramming

Spikes stress hormones and bypasses REM/slow-wave sleep stages necessary to synthesize synaptic connections.
